The recommendation argues that teachers should provide adolescents with direct and explicit instruction in comprehension strategies to improve reading comprehension. 

## Key Concepts
- **Direct and explicit instruction**: Teacher models, explains, guides practice, gives feedback, and promotes independent use of strategies.
- **Comprehension strategies**: Summarizing, asking/answering questions, paraphrasing, finding main ideas, using graphic organizers, drawing inferences, making predictions, connecting to prior knowledge.
- **Active student participation** is crucial; scaffolding must be sufficient.
- **Multiple‑strategy training** is more effective than single‑strategy training.
- **Selection of texts** matters: match strategy to text type and reading level.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Definition and Rationale**
   - Strategies are routines readers use to make sense of texts; they include summarizing, questioning, paraphrasing, finding main ideas, etc.
   - Direct instruction involves modeling, guided practice, feedback, and independent practice.
   - Evidence: five randomized experiments plus a single‑subject design study support strong efficacy (Level of evidence: Strong).
2. **Evidence Summary**
   - Studies show teaching main‑idea summarization improves comprehension of narrative and informational texts.
   - Numerous other studies (e.g., Brown et al., Dole et al., Gersten et al.) corroborate benefits of strategies such as questioning, summarizing, and graphic organizers.
   - Research indicates the *process* (active participation) may matter more than the specific strategy.
3. **Effectiveness of Specific Strategies**
   - Summarizing, questioning, and graphic organizers appear particularly powerful.
   - No conclusive ranking of strategies; cannot claim paraphrasing > summarizing, etc.
4. **Multiple‑Strategy vs. Single‑Strategy**
   - Strong studies show teaching more than one strategy yields better comprehension.
   - Examples: combined main‑idea finding + summarizing; connections + predictions + inferences.
5. **Implementation Steps for Teachers**
   1. **Select appropriate texts** – match strategy to text type (e.g., main‑idea summarizing for informational texts, not narratives).
   2. **Model strategy use across varied texts** – demonstrate flexibility.
   3. **Ensure text difficulty matches student reading level** – avoid texts too hard or too easy.
   4. **Use direct instruction sequence**:
      - State the strategy and its importance.
      - Model thinking aloud.
      - Provide guided practice with feedback.
      - Offer independent practice.
      - Discuss when/where to apply the strategy.
   5. **Adjust guided practice amount** – simpler strategies (e.g., predicting) need brief demos; complex ones (e.g., summarizing) need extended, step‑by‑step support, cooperative groups, gradual release of responsibility.

## Definitions
| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Direct and explicit instruction | Teacher‑led approach involving clear explanation, modeling, guided practice, feedback, and independent application of a skill. |
| Comprehension strategy | Routine or procedure (e.g., summarizing, questioning) that helps readers construct meaning from text. |
| Scaffolding | Temporary support provided to learners to ensure success while they acquire a new skill. |
| Multiple‑strategy training | Instruction that teaches two or more comprehension strategies to the same group of students. |
| Single‑subject design study | Research design focusing on one participant or a small group to examine intervention effects. |
