FastStats: Sleep in Adults – CDC

## Key Points
- CDC's Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS) tracks sleep duration among U.S. adults aged 18+.
- Insufficient sleep prevalence varies by geography, sex, age, and race/ethnicity.

## The Basics
- BRFSS surveys adults about health behaviors, including the question: “On average, how many hours of sleep do you get in a 24‑hour period?”
- Recommended adult sleep: **at least 7 hours per day**.
- Adults reporting **< 7 hours** are classified as having **insufficient sleep** or **short sleep duration**.

## Quick Stats
### Trends (2013–2022)
- The national percentage of adults not getting enough sleep **remained stable** over this period.

### Insufficient Sleep by State (2022)
- Range: **30 %** in Vermont (lowest) to **46 %** in Hawaii (highest).

### Insufficient Sleep by County (2020)
- Range: **24 %** in Boulder County, Colorado (lowest) to **48 %** in Greene County, Alabama (highest).

### Highest‑Risk Groups (2022)
- **Men**: 37 %
- Adults **45–64 years**: 39 %
- **Native Hawaiian or Pacific Islander** adults: 49 %

## Where the Data Comes From
### National, State, and Population Data
- Source: BRFSS (2013–2022) with age‑adjusted estimates based on the U.S. 2000 standard population.
- Note: Sleep was omitted from the core BRFSS survey in **2015, 2017, 2019, and 2021**.

### County‑Level Data
- Derived from CDC’s **PLACES** project, using model‑based estimates from:
  - BRFSS 2020
  - American Community Survey 2016–2020
  - U.S. Census 2020 county population estimates
