The Cornell Note-Taking System provides a structured two‑column layout for capturing and reviewing lecture or reading material, helping students organize main ideas, keywords, and summaries for efficient study.

## Materials / Prerequisites
- Paper or digital canvas (e.g., lined PDF, Word document, or note‑taking app) — enough space to create a 2‑column layout plus a summary area at the bottom (≈ 5 cm/2 inches high).
- Writing tools (pen/pencil) or a keyboard if using a digital tool.
- Optional: Cornell‑specific templates (e.g., Cornell Note‑taking Lined Paper PDF, Columns App, Notion or Evernote templates).

## Steps
1. **Divide the page**
   - Create a left‑hand column (≈ 1/3 of the width) for questions/keywords.
   - Create a right‑hand column (≈ 2/3 of the width) for detailed notes.
   - Leave a 5‑to‑7‑line (≈ 5 cm) section at the bottom for a summary.
2. **Take notes during the lecture or reading**
   - Write the main ideas, paraphrase long sentences, and use symbols or abbreviations in the right‑hand (note‑taking) column.
3. **Record cues**
   - As soon as possible after the session, fill the left‑hand column with questions, keywords, or cues that correspond to the notes on the right.
4. **Write a summary**
   - In the bottom section, concisely restate the core concepts of the material in your own words.
5. **Review**
   - Cover the right‑hand column, read each cue in the left column, and try to recall the detailed notes.
   - Regularly revisit the summary and cues to reinforce retention.

## Tips & Warnings
- **Tip:** Use the cue column immediately after class while the material is fresh to capture accurate keywords.
- **Tip:** Keep sentences short; rely on symbols and abbreviations to save space and speed up writing.
- **Warning:** Skipping the summary section reduces the method’s review benefit.
- **Warning:** Inconsistent formatting (e.g., uneven columns) can make later review harder.

## Result
- A compact, organized set of notes that separate key concepts from supporting details, making it easier to skim, self‑test, and retain information over time.
