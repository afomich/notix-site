The article provides a comprehensive overview of the Apollo 11 mission, covering its primary and secondary objectives, a detailed chronological timeline of key events, crew information, pre‑launch milestones, and mission specifications such as launch details, orbit parameters, and landing data.

## Overview
- **Primary objective:** Fulfill President Kennedy’s 1961 national goal of a crewed lunar landing and safe return to Earth.
- **Secondary objectives:** Conduct scientific experiments (solar wind, seismic, laser ranging), deploy a TV camera, photograph the lunar surface and equipment, and collect lunar samples.
- **Free‑return trajectory:** Last Apollo flight using a trajectory that allowed an abort back to Earth without engine firing before lunar orbit insertion.

## Mission Timeline
- **Launch:** July 16, 1969, 9:32 a.m. EDT from Cape Kennedy (Launch Pad 39A) aboard Saturn‑V AS‑506.
- **Trans‑lunar coast & mid‑course corrections:** S‑IVB stage reignited 2 h 44 min after launch; only the first of four planned mid‑course burns was required (July 17).
- **Lunar orbit insertion:** First maneuver at ~75 h 50 min (357.5 s burn) to 69 × 190 mi elliptical orbit; second 17 s burn to 62 × 70.5 mi orbit.
- **LM (Eagle) descent & landing:** LM undocked at 100 h 12 min; descent engine fired at 101 h 36 min; powered descent began at 102 h 33 min. Eagle touched down in the Sea of Tranquility at 102 h 45 min, coordinates 0° 41′ 15″ N, 23° 26′ E.
- **EVA (Moonwalk):** Armstrong stepped onto the surface at 109 h 42 min; Aldrin followed ~20 min later. EVA lasted >2.5 h, ending at 111 h 39 min.
- **Lunar surface stay:** 21 h 36 min total on the Moon; 7 h of sleep.
- **Ascent & rendezvous:** Ascent stage engine fired at 124 h 22 min; docking with Columbia occurred at 128 h 3 min.
- **Trans‑Earth injection:** July 21, SPS fired 2.5 min; only one return‑flight mid‑course correction (11.2 s) on July 22.
- **Re‑entry & splashdown:** Re‑entry procedures started July 24, 44 h after leaving lunar orbit. Splashdown at 195 h 18 min 35 s in the Pacific Ocean, 13 mi from USS Hornet (coordinates 13° 19′ N, 169° 9′ W).

## Crew
- **Neil Armstrong** – Commander
- **Buzz Aldrin** – Lunar Module Pilot
- **Michael Collins** – Command Module Pilot

## Backup Crew
- **James A. Lovell** – Commander
- **Fred W. Haise Jr.** – Lunar Module Pilot
- **William A. Anders** – Command Module Pilot

## Payload
- **Command/Service Module:** Columbia (CSM‑107)
- **Lunar Module:** Eagle (LM‑5)

## Pre‑launch Milestones (selected)
- 11/21/68 – LM‑5 integrated systems test
- 12/6/68 – CSM‑107 integrated systems test
- 1/8/69 – LM‑5 ascent stage delivered to Kennedy
- 5/20/69 – CSM electrical mate to Saturn V
- 6/26/69 – Countdown Demonstration Test

## Launch Details
- **Date/Time:** July 16, 1969; 9:32 a.m. EDT
- **Vehicle:** Saturn‑V AS‑506
- **Location:** Launch Pad 39A, Kennedy Space Center

## Orbit Parameters (Post‑lunar insertion)
- **Altitude:** 118.65 mi
- **Inclination:** 32.521°
- **Revolutions:** 30
- **Duration:** 8 days 3 h 18 min 35 s
- **Distance Traveled:** 953,054 mi
- **Lunar Location:** Sea of Tranquility (≈0.71° N, 23.63° E)

## Landing (Splashdown) Details
- **Date/Time:** July 24, 1969; 12:50 p.m. EDT
- **Location:** Pacific Ocean, 13 mi from USS Hornet
- **Recovery Ship:** USS Hornet
