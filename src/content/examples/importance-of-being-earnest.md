  FIRST ACT

  SCENE

  Morning-room in Algernon’s flat in Half-Moon Street. The room is luxuriously and artistically furnished. The sound of a piano is heard in the adjoining room.

  [Lane is arranging afternoon tea on the table, and after the music has ceased, Algernon enters.]

  ALGERNON. Did you hear what I was playing, Lane?

  LANE. I didn’t think it polite to listen, sir.

  ALGERNON. I’m sorry for that, for your sake. I don’t play accurately—any one can play accurately—but I play with wonderful expression. As far as the piano is concerned, sentiment is my forte. I keep science for Life.

## Characters and Interactions
- **Algernon** – host, witty, preoccupied with music, sandwiches, and the social proprieties of marriage. Frequently questions the servants, critiques others, and invents the fictitious invalid “Bunbury”.
- **Lane** – the butler/servant, supplies cucumber sandwiches, answers questions about champagne consumption, and reports the lack of cucumbers in the market.
- **Jack (Ernest Worthing)** – arrives as a guest, claims to be in love with Gwendolen, explains his dual identity (Ernest in town, Jack in the country) and his guardianship of Cecily Cardew.
- **Lady Bracknell** – arrives later with Gwendolen, comments on etiquette, cucumber sandwiches, and the health of the fictional invalid Bunbury.
- **Gwendolen (Miss Fairfax)** – Jack’s love interest, obsessed with the name “Ernest”, dismisses the name “Jack”, and engages in rapid, flirtatious banter.

## Plot Developments
- **Opening Banter** – Algernon and Lane discuss piano playing, cucumber sandwiches, and the consumption of champagne by servants.
- **Marriage Commentary** – Lane offers a brief, mildly cynical view of marriage; Algernon muses that the lower orders set a poor moral example.
- **Jack’s Arrival** – Jack declares his purpose to propose to Gwendolen, prompting Algernon to question his motives and to mention a mysterious “Cecily”.
- **Cigarette Case Conflict** – Algernon claims the case belongs to a “Cecily” and not to Jack; the case reveals Jack’s dual identity and his guardian relationship to Cecily Cardew.
- **Bunbury Revelation** – Algernon accuses Jack of being a “Bunburyist” (someone who creates a fictitious invalid to escape obligations). Jack explains his own invented persona (Ernest) for social convenience.
- **Social Satire** – Algernon and Jack exchange witty critiques of modern life, literature, and the absurdity of social customs.
- **Lady Bracknell’s Entrance** – She demands cucumber sandwiches, learns of the missing cucumbers, and later requests that Algernon keep the fictional Mr. Bunbury from relapsing before a Saturday event.
- **Romantic Exchanges** – Gwendolen and Jack discuss the importance of the name “Ernest”, with Gwendolen insisting only “Ernest” can be loved, while Jack argues the name is irrelevant.
- **Proposal Tension** – The scene ends with Jack poised to propose, while Gwendolen notes that no proposal has yet been made.

## Themes
- **Dual Identity** – Jack’s Ernest/Jack split and Algernon’s Bunbury illustrate the Victorian preoccupation with maintaining respectable façades.
- **Marriage and Morality** – Characters debate the value and moral impact of marriage, often with sarcastic overtones.
- **Social Satire** – The dialogue lampoons upper‑class etiquette, the role of servants, and the trivialities of high society (e.g., cucumber sandwiches, champagne consumption).
- **Language and Names** – Gwendolen’s fixation on the name “Ernest” underscores the absurdity of social conventions dictating personal affection.
